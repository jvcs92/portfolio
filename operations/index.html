Operations: making a hard-asset business profitable

I've run operations in micromobility, quick commerce and car rental. The pattern is the same: find where the money leaks, fix it with a process or a tool I build, and measure the result.

  1. Portugal's first EBITDA-positive year

    Bird · Country Manager, Portugal · 2024–2026

    The problem

    Portugal had never had an EBITDA-positive year. The goal was to grow and become profitable at the same time, in a business with expensive assets and very volatile demand.

    What I did

    • Scaled the fleet from 4,000 to 10,000+ vehicles (€100M+ in assets) while keeping unit economics in check.
    • Owned pricing, the rider lifecycle and the launch of the subscription programme.
    • Led most of the expansion, to about 30 municipalities including Lisbon, Porto, Cascais, Braga, Faro, Coimbra and Funchal.
    • Hired and developed a 50+ person organisation across operations, government partnerships and field teams.

    Result

    • First EBITDA-positive year for Bird in Portugal
    • Revenue doubled (2x)
    • Yearly rides 2.4x

  2. Regional cost of sales from 42% to 30%

    Bird · Regional Head of Operations, Iberia & France

    The problem

    Across Portugal, Spain and France, cost of sales was 42% of revenue. Some markets run in-house, others through 3PL (third-party logistics) partners on Bird-owned vehicles.

    What I did

    • Renegotiated vendor contracts and restructured 3PL contracts.
    • Put SLAs (Service Level Agreements), penalties and KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) into every vendor and 3PL contract.
    • Ran efficiency programmes with a team of 8 and a field workforce of up to 150 people.

    Result

    • Cost of sales 42% → 30%, yearly average
    • Region tracking 8% ahead of the 2026 target, driven in part by the regional pricing strategy I own

  3. A €4 part that stopped €250K a year in vandalism

    Bird · Strategy & Operations Manager, Portugal · 2022–2024

    The problem

    150 to 200 vehicle IoT (Internet of Things) units were vandalised every month, at about €120 each. That is roughly €21K a month, or €250K a year.

    What I did

    Designed a steel component that protects the IoT unit, found a supplier to produce it, and fitted it across the 4,000-vehicle fleet for about €16K in total (€4 per vehicle).

    Result

    • Vandalism incidents −90%
    • Paid back in the first month

  4. Fewer vehicles, better fleet

    Bird · Regional Head of Operations, Iberia & France

    The problem

    We couldn't procure some spare parts, so broken vehicles stayed broken and fleet quality dropped.

    What I did

    Designed a cannibalisation model that picks the lowest-performing 1% of the fleet, by ratings, ride duration, cancellations, repair frequency and depreciation, and scraps those vehicles for parts. A smaller fleet, in better condition.

    Result

    • Average fleet rating 3.9 → 4.5 out of 5
    • Cancellations and very short rides −10%

  5. 8 dark stores, profitable in 7 months

    Glovo · Operations Manager, Micro-Fulfillment Centers · 2021–2022

    The problem

    Store managers spent 6 hours a day building supplier orders by hand, instead of running the store. Orders were inaccurate, and food was bought and thrown away.

    What I did

    • Pulled sales and stock from Odoo every day and built a tool that suggests an order per supplier, based on past sales and the growth trend. The manager only reviews, corrects or adds items. New SKUs (stock keeping units) get a manual first order, and the tool takes over once there is sales history.
    • Built a second tool for near-expiry stock: if one store has products it doesn't sell, it finds the store that sells them and creates the transfer order automatically, instead of a new purchase.
    • Managers used the time they got back to lay out shelves by best-selling SKU, control fresh produce and organise pickers.

    Result

    • Ordering time 6h → 1h a day per store
    • Pick time per order 2:50 → 1:45, under the 2-minute target
    • Food waste −20%
    • Helped the 8-store network reach CM2 (Contribution Margin 2) profitability in 7 months, with an 85+ person team
