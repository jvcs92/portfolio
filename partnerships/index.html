  1. Multi-year permits in Porto, Cascais and Matosinhos

    Bird · Country Manager, Portugal, then Regional Head of Operations · B2G (Business-to-Government)

    The problem

    A permit decides whether you can operate in a city at all, and cities choose operators through negotiations and public tenders.

    What I did

    • Led the B2G negotiations and built the relationships with city regulators myself.
    • Hold Bird's relationships with mayors and mobility councillors across Portugal, and handle media requests in the country.
    • Led GBFS and MDS (General Bikeshare Feed Specification and Mobility Data Specification) data integrations with municipal and transit authorities, together with Product and Engineering.
    • Today I direct a team of Government Partnerships Managers across Portugal, Spain and France.

    Result

    • Multi-year permits in Porto, Cascais and Matosinhos
    • 10+ new city launches unlocked
    • Expansion to about 30 municipalities, including Lisbon, Oeiras, Braga, Faro, Coimbra and Funchal
    • Never lost an RFP in Portugal

  2. Transdev in Covilhã: a B2B2G deal, end to end

    Bird · Portugal · B2B2G (business-to-business-to-government): transport operator + city

    The problem

    Transdev won the city's public tender for shared mobility, but it had no scooters.

    What I did

    Handled the deal end to end: negotiation, contract terms, signing and deployment. Bird deploys 200 scooters and runs the operation for Transdev. Transdev pays a fixed monthly fee until the end of its contract with the city and covers warehouse and operating costs. Bird keeps all ride revenue.

    Result

    • One of Bird's most profitable markets
    • No costs beyond repair parts

  4. Operating partners that stay

    Bird · Engagement Manager, then Regional Head of Operations · B2B

    How the model works

    In many markets, B2B partners run Bird's street operations. Bird lends the vehicles and its fleet software and pays a share of revenue; partners operate and maintain the fleet under SLAs (Service Level Agreements) and penalties. It works like SaaS: the partners have to be kept.

    What I did

    • Cut the weakest partners. As Engagement Manager in 2022, I identified the partners that weren't performing and ended those contracts.
    • Gave the best ones more fleet. I moved their vehicles to the best-performing partners. More volume made the business more profitable and reliable for them, and let us pay less per vehicle and a smaller revenue share.
    • Kept them close. Today my Operations Associates work as customer success with each partner, under SLAs, penalties and KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) in every contract.

    Result (2022)

    • Partner churn −70%
    • Monthly vehicle churn 4% → 1.2%
    • Accelerated vehicle depreciation −75% a year
